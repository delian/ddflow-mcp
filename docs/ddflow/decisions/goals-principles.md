## The questions and the accepted defaults

Accepted as written on 2026-10-13 (recommended defaults, verbatim from the task body):

QUESTIONS AND RECOMMENDED DEFAULTS (evidence: R-goals-existing-map, R-goals-principles-proposals-design):
1. Is a principle a new kind or a field? DEFAULT: a field, not a kind. Add one field to the guidance record, override = none | waive | deviate (how a finding is cleared), orthogonal to enforcement (how hard a finding is). Principle = enforcement warn + override deviate; rule = block + waive; org floor = block + none; preference = advisory. Vocabulary shown to people: MUST / SHOULD / MAY (RFC 2119) next to the enforcement word. Rejected: a separate principle store (a second engine to keep in step), and enforcement alone (a warn finding needs no answer, so a soft rule fails by silence).
2. Who may deviate from a principle, and does it expire? DEFAULT: the authoring agent records a deviation, item-scoped, reason required (evidence optional), no approval, no expiry (it dies with the item), always visible in brief, completion summary and the operator's decision list. Only block rules need a person's waiver (up to 90 days, approved by digest, unchanged). A deviation from a principle on a CHECK-LESS record (judged by a reviewer) is accepted the same way.
3. Must a principle finding be answered before complete? DEFAULT: yes for SHOULD with a mechanical check or a reviewer finding that cites the id; complete refuses (exit 3) listing the unanswered ones; an answer is comply (fixed) or deviate (reason). MAY findings never refuse. Gate OUTCOME stays passed (a principle is not a blocker); the ledger carries the answer.
4. Precedence order. DEFAULT: org floor > project rule (block) > accepted decision > principle > preference; nearest scope (gate, then globs, then category, then always) wins within a tier, then priority, then newest; unreconciled conflicts are a doctor note and a B-coh item, never a silent pick. Goals never override a rule or a principle. Extends D-rules-hierarchy (workflow enforcement > project rules > agent defaults); it does not replace it.
5. Goal record shape. DEFAULT: def-record kind goal; fields title, body, status (proposed | active | achieved | dropped), owner, target_date, parent (at most 2 levels), links (phases, tasks, decisions, research), key_results[] each {id, text, source = dag | metric | check | manual, ref, baseline, target, direction}. Progress is computed: dag = done share of the linked phases' tasks (tasks weighted equally; a removed or abandoned task leaves the count); metric = a named ddflow metric (B-gap-metrics); check = a command through the shared runner, operator-config or approved by digest only; manual = an operator attestation with a date that goes STALE after [goals].manual_stale_days (default 30) and is then reported unavailable, never green. Overall goal progress = the minimum-of-trajectory view is NOT used; show each KR and their mean, marked an estimate.
6. What does the brief show of goals? DEFAULT: at most 3 lines (active goals by priority: title, mean KR progress, weakest KR, days to target, stale flag), plus at claim the item's goal chain (task -> phase -> goal) labelled Direction, not a constraint; orphan work (completed items under no goal) counted in the roadmap and doctor, never a gate.
7. Proposal storage and triage states. DEFAULT: def-record kind proposal outside the queue (status proposed | adopted | deferred (snooze_until) | rejected | expired | superseded); adopt creates a task through services/items.add_task (dup check included) in a phase the operator names and links back; adopt/defer/reject are person-only (CLI; no MCP verb, like ddflow approve); reject REQUIRES a reason and is remembered: a new proposal whose fingerprint or similarity matches a rejected one is not filed unless it carries new evidence the operator can see. Rejected: proposal as a task state (every scheduler, board and next path would need to learn it), and proposal as a research record (no adopt/reject lifecycle).
8. Discovery cadence and budget. DEFAULT: OFF until the operator turns it on (job feature_discovery shipped disabled); then weekly by the calendar, or every 2 completed phases, whichever the operator picks; per run at most 3 new proposals; at most 7 open (proposed, undecided) at any time, and the job does not run while the cap is reached (backpressure); at most 20 000 reviewed tokens per run when B-gap-spend can measure it (UNAVAILABLE, not zero, when it cannot, and then the run cap is the turn cap); circuit breaker after 3 consecutive runs with zero adopted proposals (job pauses and says so in brief); stale undecided proposals expire after 60 days. The job files ONE research item (kind research) through the normal claim, so any lane may take it and quotas apply.
9. What may discovery read? DEFAULT: the repository, the ddflow log, its own research records, dependency advisories through the configured companions; the web ONLY if the operator enabled [discovery].web (default false); the log redaction profile is applied to every proposal body; no file contents above 40 lines are quoted. Org policy may force web off.
10. Scope. DEFAULT: goals and principles may come from an org bundle (P-org-config) as read-only team goals/principles; a project may add its own and cannot deviate from an org floor; proposals and deviations are always local to the project log; personal (local-only) goals are allowed under .ddflow/local and never reach the brief of other users.
11. Evaluation gate for turning discovery on by default. DEFAULT: never on by default; recommend turning on after B-goal-eval reports >= 3 runs with acceptance in 20-70 percent.
